# Research gaps and next investigations

1. **Fitzpatrick V–VI enrolment** remains limited in many cited studies, especially randomized light-response trials.
2. **Measured melanin content** is often missing; most racial/ethnic comparisons cannot identify pigment as the causal mechanism.
3. **African and African-diaspora clinical cohorts** deserve well-powered prospective studies that reflect actual environmental exposure, photoprotection use and cosmetic routines. Local partnerships, participant consent and transparent reporting are essential.
4. **Transferable formulation parameters** (vehicle, pH, stability, application amount and cumulative exposure) are often not sufficiently standardized for ingredient-to-ingredient ranking.
5. **Visible-light spectral attenuation** is not captured by an SPF number and should be part of pigmentation-focused photoprotection design.
6. **Clinical endpoints** should distinguish existing PIH correction, induced pigmentation prevention, acne treatment, skin irritation and objective barrier change.
7. **Long-term adverse effects and adherence** need more research in highly melanated skin, including shade compatibility and cosmetic acceptability.
8. **Independent replication** is particularly valuable where products are sponsor-associated or ingredients are patented.

## Suggested next research protocol

A prospective randomized trial comparing test and matched vehicle across objectively measured melanin-index strata, with pre-registered primary endpoint, effect-size/CI reporting, a prespecified interaction test, adequate representation of V–VI, instrument-calibrated photography, standardized exposures and independent statistical oversight. This is a design concept, not a finalized clinical protocol.
