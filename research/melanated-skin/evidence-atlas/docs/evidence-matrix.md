# Evidence matrix: what can be inferred?

| Problem | Papers | Comparative population evidence? | Practical hypothesis | Caveat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tyrosinase responses | MS001, MS004 | Yes, but limited | Compare biomarker and clinical endpoints by melanin index | Early mechanistic evidence does not establish longer-term patient benefit |
| Irritant surfactants | MS002, MS006, MS008 | Yes, using population labels | Test TEWL, colour changes and stinging separately | Ethnicity ≠ measured melanin |
| Retinoid tolerability | MS003, MS006, MS007 | Yes, with heterogeneous designs | Analyse dryness, erythema, PIH and adherence as different outcomes | Cohort confounding and patch-test-to-product extrapolation |
| Glycolic acid and UV | MS005 | Yes, small study | Evaluate post-exfoliation pigmentation under ethically designed test conditions | Small samples; not general sunscreen guidance |
| Tinted photoprotection | MS009, MS010 | No direct light-versus-dark comparison | Include spectral visible-light testing and accessible shade range | Iron-oxide product performance is formulation-specific |
| Visible-light active prevention | MS011 | No, III–IV trials | Separate prevention of HEV-induced darkening from correction of spots | Vitamin C null result applies to one studied endpoint and vehicle |
| Pigment-correcting actives | MS012, MS013 | No | Compare vehicle-controlled results on PIH/lesional skin | Results do not rank all possible actives across all skin types |

## Important null-result distinction

A lack of statistical significance in a subgroup is not proof that the subgroup biologically fails to respond. The relevant test for a *different response* is a formal cross-group interaction, accompanied by an effect-size estimate and uncertainty. The repository intentionally phrases the Boissy and other subgroup findings conservatively.
