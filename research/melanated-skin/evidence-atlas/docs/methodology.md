# Evidence methodology and interpretation rules

**Scope:** Peer-reviewed papers from the curated conversation list, bibliographically checked against PubMed or the publisher on 2026-11-25. This is a *focused research atlas*, **not** a comprehensive or systematic search of all dermatology trials. Absence here is not evidence of ineffectiveness.

## Classification

- **Across population groups:** the same intervention was compared between self-reported or reported ethnic/racial groups, or between light- and dark-skin groupings. Such designs answer questions about *observed group difference*, not necessarily the causal role of melanin.
- **Formulations within darker phototypes:** active, vehicle or sunscreen regimen comparisons conducted in predominantly Fitzpatrick III–VI/IV–VI participants. No lighter-skin comparator was present.
- **Actives within darker phototypes:** different actives or controls compared in those populations. Results cannot be treated as direct light-versus-dark response differences.
- **Treatment vs vehicle within darker phototypes:** establishes an outcome in a studied skin group, not a between-phototype effect.

**Rule:** Assign `Across population groups` only when different population groups are actually compared. Never label a within-group formulation trial as evidence of a melanin-specific response difference. Even a statistically significant treatment effect in only one subgroup does **not** establish a significant treatment-by-subgroup interaction unless it was directly tested.

## Appraisal dimensions

For every study, check: design (prospective, retrospective, experimental), total and per-group n, measured phototype or melanin index, exact intervention/vehicle, comparator, clinical vs surrogate endpoints, effect size and uncertainty, follow-up, multiplicity, industry relationships, external validity, and safety reporting.

### Terminology

- **Fitzpatrick phototype**: categorical burning/tanning propensity; not a quantitative proxy for epidermal melanin density.
- **Ethnicity/race**: broad social/population labels. These variables cannot be substituted for melanin index and do not independently determine a formulation response.
- **PIH**: post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation; a clinically important endpoint distinct from skin lightening of unaffected tissue.
- **TEWL**: transepidermal water loss; indicator used to study barrier function.
- **L\***: CIELAB lightness value, to be interpreted with study design and lesion/location controls.
- **Vehicle**: inactive delivery system; can alter permeability, irritation and apparent efficacy.

## Reproducibility checklist

1. Confirm PMID and DOI against PubMed and publisher record.
2. Read methods, figures, subgroup denominators, comparator and disclosures from full text when available.
3. Record the exact intervention formulation, percentage, volume applied, frequency and route, **only if actually reported**.
4. Distinguish statistically significant *within-arm change* from a statistically significant *between-arm difference*.
5. Record effect size and confidence interval wherever available, not p-value alone.
6. Extract adverse events, protocol deviations, exclusions and skin phototypes actually represented.
7. Mark unresolved fields `Not confirmed`, never fabricate sample size or dose.
8. Reassess regulatory and safety status separately from efficacy papers.

## Confidence levels

We **do not** assign faux quantitative scores. `status` describes whether bibliographic/abstract-level details have been checked, not a validated risk-of-bias grade. The catalogue may contain promising preliminary evidence that still requires full-text review or larger replication.
