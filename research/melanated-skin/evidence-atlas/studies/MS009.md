# MS009 · Impact of Iron-Oxide Containing Formulations Against Visible Light-Induced Skin Pigmentation in Skin of Color Individuals

**Citation:** Dumbuya et al. (2020). *Journal of Drugs in Dermatology.*  
**Primary source:** [PubMed PMID 32726103](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32726103/)  
**Evidence classification:** Formulations within darker phototypes  
**Source check:** PubMed and publisher excerpts checked (reviewed 2026-11-25)

## What the paper found

Iron-oxide-containing test formulations reduced induced pigmentation compared with untreated skin and the tested mineral SPF 50+ formula lacking iron oxides.

## Interpretation for formulation research

Measure visible-light coverage in addition to UV SPF when developing pigmentation-oriented tinted sunscreens.

**What this study cannot establish:** One phototype and specific tested products; sunscreen SPF alone does not measure visible-light attenuation; do not infer all iron-oxide formulas perform equally.

**Appraisal flags:** Product-specific result; may involve industry-sponsored products.

## Follow-up research question

How would the outcome change after controlling for melanin index, the actual finished-product vehicle, exposure, baseline barrier function, pigmentation endpoints and a pre-specified between-group contrast where appropriate?
