# MS006 · Ethnic differences in objective and subjective skin irritation response: an international study

**Citation:** Lee et al. (2014). *Skin Research and Technology.*  
**Primary source:** [PubMed PMID 24033862](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24033862/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/srt.12111)  
**Evidence classification:** Across population groups  
**Source check:** PubMed abstract checked (reviewed 2026-11-25)

## Study at a glance

| Dimension | Details |

| Research theme | Barrier and irritation |

| Population | Asian and Caucasian participants in four countries |

| Exposure / follow-up | Standardized challenge tests |
| Endpoints | Irritation scores, positive patch reactions and self-reported stinging |

## What the paper found

Asian participants scored higher for SLS irritation, showed greater lactic-acid stinging and tended toward more positive retinol reactions; self-perceived sensitivity was similar.

## Interpretation for formulation research

Use both observer-rated irritation and participant-reported stinging in multicultural tolerability studies.

**What this study cannot establish:** Ethnicity is not synonymous with skin pigmentation; provocation tests do not establish performance of leave-on finished products.

**Appraisal flags:** Ethnicity confounding; standardized irritants not market formulations.

## Follow-up research question

How would the outcome change after controlling for melanin index, the actual finished-product vehicle, exposure, baseline barrier function, pigmentation endpoints and a pre-specified between-group contrast where appropriate?
