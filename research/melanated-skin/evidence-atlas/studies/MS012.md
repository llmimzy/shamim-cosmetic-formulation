# MS012 · Comparative Study on Depigmenting Agents in Skin of Color

**Citation:** Liyanage et al. (2022). *Journal of Clinical and Aesthetic Dermatology.*  
**Primary source:** [PubMed PMID 35309879](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35309879/) · [Open-access full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8884189/) · No DOI assigned  
**Evidence classification:** Actives within darker phototypes  
**Source check:** PubMed abstract checked (reviewed 2026-11-25)

## Study at a glance

| Dimension | Details |
|---|---|
| Intervention(s) | Arbutin 5%; azelaic acid 20%; hydroquinone 4%; resveratrol 1%; ascorbyl palmitate 1% |
| Research theme | Pigmentation |
| Study type | Two-phase prospective formulation study |
| Population | Two groups of healthy Sri Lankan female volunteers |
| Phototypes | IV–V |
| Reported sample | 54 total (28 commercial products; 26 single-active formulations) |
| Exposure / follow-up | 6 weeks |
| Endpoints | Mexameter melanin index relative to controls |

## What the paper found

Within single-active lipophilic dispersions, only 5% arbutin was significantly different from the inactive control; some commercial products also outperformed untreated sites.

## Interpretation for formulation research

Control for vehicle and exposure before claiming a depigmenting ingredient is superior.

**What this study cannot establish:** Small single-centre short study; separate phases; does not prove universal ingredient ranking or establish general treatment guidance.

**Appraisal flags:** Vehicle-dependent results; no cross-phototype comparison.

## Follow-up research question

How would the outcome change after controlling for melanin index, the actual finished-product vehicle, exposure, baseline barrier function, pigmentation endpoints and a pre-specified between-group contrast where appropriate?
